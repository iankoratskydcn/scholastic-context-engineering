# Subtopic 04 — Context Compression and Reasoning-Relevant Context Selection

## 1. LLMLingua: Compressing Prompts for Accelerated Inference of Large Language Models

Jiang et al. (EMNLP 2023) propose LLMLingua, a coarse-to-fine prompt compressor that uses a budget controller to allocate compression across prompt components, demonstration-level reduction to preserve semantic integrity, iterative token-level compression to model conditional dependencies among retained tokens, and instruction tuning to align the small compressor with the target LLM. Across GSM8K, BBH, ShareGPT, and Arxiv-March23, the paper reports up to 20× compression with little performance loss, including strong preservation of reasoning and in-context-learning behavior. Its main limitation is that importance is estimated from token-level likelihood and compression budgets rather than from explicit claims, premises, warrants, proof steps, or logical dependencies; compression can therefore preserve task behavior without exposing why a span is inferentially necessary. It is foundational prior art for efficient prompt shortening and overlaps with a proposed reasoning-aware system at the selection/compression stage, but leaves a clear gap at the level of an inspectable argument representation and provenance-preserving dependency selection. Verified source: https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.825/

## 2. LongLLMLingua: Accelerating and Enhancing LLMs in Long Context Scenarios via Prompt Compression

Jiang et al. (ACL 2024) extend LLMLingua to long-context settings with query-aware coarse- and fine-grained compression, document reordering, dynamic compression ratios, and subsequence recovery, targeting the interaction between key-information density, position bias, cost, and latency. Experiments across long-context question answering, multi-document reasoning, and related benchmarks show substantial gains: for example, the paper reports up to a 21.4% improvement on NaturalQuestions with roughly four times fewer GPT-3.5-Turbo input tokens, 94% cost reduction on LooGLE, and 1.4–2.6× latency acceleration for approximately 10k-token prompts compressed 2–6×. The limitation is that its query-conditioned importance signal is based on model likelihood/contrastive perplexity and positional or document-level heuristics, not on an explicit logical form or argument graph. It therefore overlaps with reasoning-aware context selection through query conditioning and protected information density, while leaving open the extraction of claims, premises, warrants, support/attack relations, and other inferential dependencies before compression. Verified source: https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.91/

## 3. LLMLingua-2: Data Distillation for Efficient and Faithful Task-Agnostic Prompt Compression

Pan et al. (Findings ACL 2024) recast prompt compression as token classification: an LLM generates compression supervision, and a smaller bidirectional encoder is distilled to decide which tokens to retain or remove using full contextual information. The resulting compressor is faster and more faithful than repeatedly invoking a large autoregressive model, and the paper reports strong task-agnostic compression performance across multiple benchmarks. The central limitation is representational: the classifier predicts token retention labels rather than identifying argument components, logical relations, proof dependencies, or source-grounded propositions, so it may retain a useful lexical surface while discarding a low-frequency but globally essential premise. LLMLingua-2 is the closest overlap for a system that classifies before selecting context, and it establishes token-level classification as prior art; the gap is an explicit reasoning-structure intermediate representation whose selection objective preserves inferential dependencies rather than only token utility. Verified source: https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-acl.57/

## 4. RECOMP: Improving Retrieval-Augmented LMs with Compression and Selective Augmentation

Xu, Shi, and Choi (ICLR 2024) introduce RECOMP, a retrieval-context compressor trained with downstream language-model performance as the objective, with both extractive and abstractive variants and the ability to return an empty string when retrieved evidence is not useful. The method improves retrieval-augmented generation by reducing distractors and selectively augmenting the generator with evidence that helps answer the task, demonstrating that compression can be learned as an end-task utility decision rather than as generic summarization. Its limitation is that extractive selection operates at the sentence level and abstractive compression generates a summary; neither explicitly identifies claims, premises, warrants, evidence roles, support/attack relations, or a logical skeleton. RECOMP overlaps with a proposed approach in downstream, task-oriented selection and in the possibility of dropping irrelevant evidence entirely, but it leaves the argument-aware representation and provenance-preserving reasoning selection problem unresolved. Verified source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.04408/

## 5. Compressing Context to Enhance Inference Efficiency of Large Language Models (Selective Context)

Li et al. (EMNLP 2023) present Selective Context, a model-agnostic method that removes redundant lexical units—tokens, phrases, or sentences—using surprisal and information-based criteria, then evaluates the shortened context on long documents, news, and conversations. The paper provides a simple and broadly applicable baseline for reducing input length while retaining generation quality and faithfulness, making explicit that local information content can guide inference-time pruning without retraining the target model. Its limitation is the mismatch between local redundancy and global reasoning value: a premise may be locally predictable or low-surprisal yet indispensable to a conclusion, and the method does not recover argument roles or dependencies. Selective Context is therefore important baseline prior art for lexical pruning, but a reasoning-aware compressor would need to add structure extraction, dependency protection, and source-span provenance rather than relying only on token- or sentence-level information scores. Verified source: https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.391/

## 6. Adapting Language Models to Compress Contexts (AutoCompressors)

Chevalier et al. (EMNLP 2023) train language models to convert long text segments into compact continuous summary vectors or soft prompts, recursively passing compressed memory forward so later computation can condition on earlier content without rereading the full text. The paper demonstrates that latent compressed context can support long-context language modeling, compressed demonstrations, retrieval-augmented use, and passage reranking, showing that raw text is not always necessary at inference time. Its key limitation is opacity: the latent vectors have no directly inspectable argument units, logical relations, or span-level provenance, and compression is representational rather than an explicit extract-then-select pipeline. AutoCompressors thus overlap with a proposed system in the goal of replacing bulky context with compact memory, but they leave a gap for auditable reasoning scaffolds in which each retained proposition and dependency can be traced back to source text. Verified source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14788

## 7. In-context Autoencoder for Context Compression in a Large Language Model (ICAE)

Ge et al. (ICLR 2024) introduce ICAE, a lightweight compressor that maps long contexts into compact memory slots consumed directly by a base LLM, using autoencoding/language-modeling pretraining followed by instruction tuning. The method reports substantial reductions in context length, latency, and GPU-memory use while retaining useful task behavior, establishing a practical latent-memory route that does not require changing the full target model. Its limitation is that the memory slots are opaque and there is no explicit extraction of claims, premises, warrants, proof steps, or logical relations, nor a selective retention stage that identifies which source spans support the downstream answer. ICAE is strong prior art for latent context compression and efficient inference, but it does not subsume an argument-skeleton approach whose contribution is an inspectable, provenance-aware intermediate representation and reasoning-specific selection objective. Verified source: https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/0b276510ec2d3f6613a8b60c41ff0438-Abstract-Conference.html

## 8. Learning to Compress Prompts with Gist Tokens

Mu et al. (NeurIPS 2023) propose gist tokens, a small set of learned tokens into which a model encodes prompts, instructions, or demonstrations so that the compressed representation can be reused in subsequent generation. Experiments with decoder-only and encoder-decoder models show large prompt-length reductions while preserving task behavior, making gist tokens a reusable primitive for few-shot examples and repeated instructions. The limitation is that the representation is learned end to end and is not required to expose or preserve explicit argument components, logical relations, negation, numerical qualifiers, or provenance links to the original prompt. Gist tokens overlap with any proposed method that serializes selected reasoning information into a compact context, but they do not establish argument-aware selection; the open gap is whether an inspectable logical or argumentative scaffold can provide better faithfulness and controllability than opaque learned tokens. Verified source: https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/3d77c6dcc7f143aa2154e7f4d5e22d68-Abstract.html

## 9. SelfCP: Compressing Over-Limit Prompt via the Frozen Large Language Model Itself

Gao, Cao, and Li (2024) propose SelfCP, which uses the target frozen LLM to encode over-limit prompt regions into dense vectors and a small learnable connector and embedding layer to convert those vectors into dense tokens, while leaving the allowed portion of the prompt unmodified. Training updates only the lightweight connector and uses language-model supervision on long texts plus instruction data; experiments with two backbones report substituting roughly 12× over-limit prompts with dense tokens, reducing memory costs and improving throughput while maintaining or improving response quality. Its limitation is that the compression boundary is driven by hard context overflow and positional partitioning, while the dense tokens remain opaque and lack argument-level provenance or logical dependency information. SelfCP overlaps with hybrid designs that preserve some raw context while compressing overflow, but it leaves open a reasoning-aware policy for deciding which claims or dependencies deserve verbatim protection and which can be compressed. Verified source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.17052

## 10. Concise and Precise Context Compression for Tool-Using Language Models

Xu et al. (Findings ACL 2024) address tool-documentation compression with two complementary strategies: selective compression retains execution-critical tool and parameter names as raw tokens, while block compression partitions documentation and maps blocks to fixed-length soft summaries, allowing the effective compression ratio to vary with documentation length. On API-Bank and APIBench, the approach reaches performance comparable to an upper-bound baseline under compression ratios of up to 16×, demonstrating that mixed raw-plus-soft context is useful when exact identifiers must survive compression. The limitation is domain-specificity: protected spans are schema-critical names rather than claims, premises, warrants, or proof dependencies, and the method does not extract an argumentative or logical representation. It offers a valuable design analogy and partial precedent for protecting reasoning-critical spans exactly, but the gap is a principled mechanism for identifying those spans from inferential structure rather than tool schemas. Verified source: https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-acl.974/

## 11. Compressed Context Memory for Online Language Model Interaction

Kim et al. (ICLR 2024) propose a recurrent key/value-memory compressor for online interaction, continually folding accumulated Transformer attention keys and values into a compact memory using a lightweight conditional LoRA inserted into the forward pass, without fine-tuning all model weights. The method is trained through a parallelized formulation of recursive compression and performs at the level of a full-context model with approximately 5× smaller context memory; it also supports streaming interaction with unbounded context and outperforms a sliding-window baseline. Its limitation is that compression occurs inside model state rather than through an inspectable textual or symbolic representation, so users cannot audit which propositions were retained or recover source-span provenance. This is important prior art for persistent and streaming context engineering, but it does not overlap with an explicit extract/classify/select argument pipeline beyond sharing the general objective of preserving useful history under a memory budget. Verified source: https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/97dc07f1253ab33ee514f395a82fa7cc-Abstract-Conference.html

## 12. Effective In-Context Example Selection through Data Compression

Sun et al. (Findings ACL 2024) frame few-shot example selection as data compression and introduce a two-stage method: BM25 first recalls examples relevant to the query, then an influence-aware reranking stage uses a meta-gradient/Fisher approximation to select examples that preserve useful information from the broader training set. Across five real-world datasets and four GPT-2 model sizes, the method reports an average improvement of 5.90% and consistently outperforms its BM25 comparison under three- and four-example settings. Its limitation is that it compresses the example set rather than the internal content of each example, and the selection signal is relevance plus parameter influence rather than argument roles, logical form, or proof dependency. The paper is relevant precedent for compact reasoning-scaffold selection at the demonstration level, but it leaves a gap for extracting and ranking the reasoning-critical components within examples or source documents before serialization into the final LLM context. Verified source: https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-acl.50/

## Cross-paper conclusion

The twelve papers establish six main compression families: lexical extractive pruning, query- or task-aware token/sentence selection, abstractive textual compression, learned latent or soft-token memory, recurrent key/value-memory compression, and example-set selection. Their common limitation for the present research question is that they optimize likelihood, redundancy, task loss, retrieval utility, memory efficiency, or end-task performance without first recovering an explicit, inspectable argument or logical representation. LLMLingua-2 provides the closest classification precedent, LongLLMLingua and RECOMP provide strong query/task-conditioned selection precedents, and the tool-documentation work provides a useful protected-span design pattern; however, no verified paper in this set combines claim/premise/warrant or logical-form extraction with downstream compression whose objective is to preserve inferential dependencies for LLM reasoning. A differentiated system should therefore make the structure representation, dependency-aware selection objective, and source-span provenance central, and should compare against these methods with ablations for structure extraction, edge preservation, protected qualifiers, and provenance.
