# Subtopic 08 — Logic–LM Hybrids and Theorem Provers: Paper Summaries

Research date: 2026-10-11. Each retained paper below was opened at its ACL Anthology, ICLR Proceedings, or arXiv source page; the Explanation-Refiner paper was additionally checked from the published PDF. The novelty role is stated specifically against the question of whether prior systems classify or extract argument forms for context selection or context construction.

## 1. Logic-LM: Empowering Large Language Models with Symbolic Solvers for Faithful Logical Reasoning — Pan, Albalak, Wang, and Wang (2023)

[ACL Anthology source](https://aclanthology.org/2023.findings-emnlp.248/) · [arXiv source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.12295). Logic-LM uses an LLM to translate a natural-language logical problem into a symbolic formulation, sends that formulation to a deterministic symbolic solver for inference, and feeds solver error messages back into an iterative self-refinement loop. The framework is evaluated on ProofWriter, PrOntoQA, FOLIO, LogicalDeduction, and AR-LSAT, where solver-backed reasoning improves over ordinary prompting baselines. For novelty analysis, Logic-LM is a strong adjacent threat because it constructs a formal reasoning context from premises and a conclusion and validates the resulting inference externally; however, the verified paper does not make argument-form classification, proof-relevant sentence ranking, or minimal natural-language context selection a distinct component. A contribution focused on selecting or constructing context from candidate prose must therefore distinguish its relevance objective and selection representation from Logic-LM’s whole-problem formalization-and-repair pipeline.

## 2. LINC: A Neurosymbolic Approach for Logical Reasoning by Combining Language Models with First-Order Logic Provers — Olausson et al. (2023)

[ACL Anthology source](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.313/) · [arXiv source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.15164). LINC, or Logical Inference via Neurosymbolic Computation, treats the LLM as a semantic parser that converts natural-language premises and conclusions into first-order-logic expressions, after which an external theorem prover performs deductive inference. The authors report gains on FOLIO and a balanced subset of ProofWriter across multiple language models, demonstrating that separating semantic parsing from symbolic deduction can improve logical reliability. Its role in novelty threat analysis is a strong adjacent precedent for extracting the content of an argument into formal premises and a formal conclusion, so claims about LLM-to-FOL argument extraction cannot be presented as new without a sharper distinction. LINC does not, in the verified description, introduce a dedicated taxonomy of argument forms or a proof-relevance stage that chooses a minimal or sufficient subset of natural-language candidate sentences for context construction.

## 3. Verification and Refinement of Natural Language Explanations through LLM-Symbolic Theorem Proving (Explanation-Refiner) — Quan, Valentino, Dennis, and Freitas (2024)

[ACL Anthology source](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.172/) · [published PDF](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.172.pdf) · [arXiv source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.01379). Explanation-Refiner is the closest verified prior art for natural-language context construction: an LLM generates a rough inference, elicits sufficient and necessary facts, distinguishes relevant, redundant, and unrelated explanatory sentences, formalizes selected facts as axioms, and uses Isabelle/HOL theorem-proving feedback to iteratively refine the explanation. The framework combines event semantics and first-order logic and is evaluated on e-SNLI, QASC, and WorldTree, with the prover supplying formal validity guarantees and error feedback. In novelty analysis, this paper is the strongest direct threat because it operationalizes proof-relevant sentence selection and turns the selected explanatory context into a verified formal theory rather than merely translating an entire input problem. Any new system claiming argument-form classification or extraction for context selection must specify what is different in its argument-form taxonomy, representation, selection objective, proof/context interface, domain, or evaluation, and must compare directly against Explanation-Refiner.

## 4. LeanDojo: Theorem Proving with Retrieval-Augmented Language Models — Yang et al. (2023)

[arXiv source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.15626). LeanDojo is an open-source Lean environment, dataset, toolkit, and benchmark that extracts proof data programmatically and annotates which premises are used in formal proofs. Building on those annotations, its ReProver system retrieves accessible premises from a large Lean mathematical library and conditions an LLM-based prover on the retrieved material; the benchmark contains 98,734 theorems and proofs and includes splits requiring generalization to novel premises. Its novelty role is a direct context-selection threat when “context construction” includes retrieving formal lemmas or premises for a theorem prover: it provides a mature mechanism for selecting compact proof context from a formal library. The overlap is weaker for a contribution that classifies or extracts argument forms from natural-language prose, because LeanDojo’s inputs are formal proof states and library declarations, not candidate explanatory sentences or unstructured arguments.

## 5. Magnushammer: A Transformer-Based Approach to Premise Selection — Mikuła et al. (2024)

[ICLR Proceedings source](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/ab5f5f22e3e09f4424592ffb06840ab0-Abstract-Conference.html) · [arXiv source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.04488). Magnushammer applies contrastive transformer training to premise selection for interactive automated theorem proving, replacing hand-engineered symbolic retrieval with learned retrieval of premises relevant to a proof state. The paper reports improvements over Sledgehammer on PISA and miniF2f, further improves proof success when combined with a language-model prover, and releases a large Isabelle dataset of proof-state/relevant-premise pairs. For novelty analysis, it is a direct formal-premise-selection precedent: a claim that a new system constructs prover context by learning which formal premises matter must explain its difference from Magnushammer’s retrieval objective, training data, proof assistant, and evaluation. It is only an indirect threat to natural-language argument-form extraction because it selects from formal library premises and does not classify argumentative relations or relevance among ordinary-language sentences.

## 6. Towards Large Language Models as Copilots for Theorem Proving in Lean (Lean Copilot) — Song, Yang, and Anandkumar (2024)

[arXiv source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.12534). Lean Copilot integrates LLM inference natively into Lean and provides a framework for proof-step suggestion, proof-goal completion, and relevant-premise selection, supporting both pretrained models and user-supplied models running locally or in the cloud. The reported Mathematics in Lean experiments show improvements over rule-based automation such as aesop for both human-assisted and automated proving. Its role in novelty threat analysis is a direct baseline for interactive formal-context selection and for claims that LLMs can identify premises relevant to a Lean goal. The overlap is limited when the proposed contribution begins with natural-language argument forms, because Lean Copilot operates inside an already formalized Lean goal and library environment rather than extracting, classifying, or pruning candidate prose before formalization.

## 7. Harnessing the Power of Large Language Models for Natural Language to First-Order Logic Translation (MALLS/LogicLLaMA) — Yang et al. (2024)

[ACL Anthology source](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.375/). This paper introduces MALLS, a 28K sentence-level dataset of verified natural-language/first-order-logic pairs, created through FOL rule parsing, human annotation, and automatic filtering, and trains LogicLLaMA models based on LLaMA2 for NL-to-FOL translation and correction. The dataset and models target the fine-grained formalization bottleneck that affects solver-augmented reasoning, including correction of rules generated by other LLMs. In novelty analysis, MALLS/LogicLLaMA is an important component-level precedent for sentence-level argument or logical-form extraction: it weakens claims that translating individual natural-language statements into formal rules is itself novel. The verified source does not establish an end-to-end mechanism that classifies argument forms, selects proof-relevant sentences, constructs a minimal context for a target conclusion, or validates the selected context with a theorem prover, so the threat is moderate and mainly concerns the extraction layer.

## 8. LOGIC-LM++: Multi-Step Refinement for Symbolic Formulations — Kirtania, Gupta, and Radhakrishnan (2024)

[arXiv source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.02514). Logic-LM++ extends Logic-LM by using multi-step refinement and pairwise LLM comparisons to evaluate candidate symbolic formulations and select better refinements for complex reasoning tasks. The abstract reports improvements over Logic-LM and other methods on FOLIO, ProofWriter, and AR-LSAT. Its novelty role is an adjacent refinement-and-selection threat: it shows that comparing alternative formalizations and retaining a better candidate can be part of a logic–LM pipeline, so a proposed selection mechanism should not describe candidate-formulation comparison as unprecedented. The overlap remains narrower than explicit argument-form/context construction because Logic-LM++ selects among formal specifications generated for a problem; the verified source does not identify a natural-language argument-form classifier, a proof-relevance ranking over candidate sentences, or a minimal-context objective.

## 9. Automated Theorem Provers Help Improve Large Language Model Reasoning — McGinness and Baumgartner (2024)

[arXiv source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.03492) · [LPAR 2024 reference](https://doi.org/10.29007/2n9m). This work uses an LLM as a front end that translates PRONTOQA steamroller problems into a formal logic language, invokes logic-programming systems and first-order automated theorem provers, and analyzes translation failures using syntactic and semantic error categories. Its main extension uses theorem provers to correct semantic translation errors, reducing those errors and improving reasoning accuracy. For novelty analysis, it is a correction and validation precedent: it threatens claims that prover feedback can diagnose or repair faulty formalized arguments, and it establishes the importance of separating translation correctness from downstream proving. The verified paper does not report a dedicated argument-form classifier or a context selector that chooses relevant natural-language facts; its system formalizes the given problem and repairs errors, so the overlap with selective context construction is moderate and indirect.

## 10. LeanReasoner: Boosting Complex Logical Reasoning with Lean — Jiang, Fonseca, and Cohen (2024)

[ACL Anthology source](https://aclanthology.org/2024.naacl-long.416/). LeanReasoner formalizes logical reasoning problems as Lean theorems and solves them by proving or disproving the corresponding formal statements, leveraging Lean’s symbolic checking and theorem library. The authors report state-of-the-art performance on FOLIO and near-state-of-the-art performance on ProofWriter while using fewer than 100 in-domain samples per dataset. Its novelty role is a formal-context and proof-assistant baseline: it shows that converting natural-language logical tasks into a theorem-prover representation and relying on library-backed symbolic verification is established practice. The overlap is weaker for argument-form classification or selective context construction because the abstract describes theorem formalization and proof/disproof rather than a separate stage that extracts argumentative relations, ranks candidate facts, or constructs a minimal proof-relevant context.

## 11. Language Models can be Deductive Solvers (LoGiPT) — Feng et al. (2024)

[ACL Anthology source](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-naacl.254/) · [arXiv source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.06158). LoGiPT addresses a weakness of solver-augmented language models in which an LLM’s parsing error prevents the external solver from executing: instead of relying on an explicit parser-to-solver interface, it trains a language model to internalize and emulate deductive-solver reasoning while adhering to strict solver syntax and grammar. The model is trained on a dataset derived from revealed and refined solver reasoning and is evaluated on two deductive reasoning benchmarks, outperforming solver-augmented and few-shot alternatives in the reported experiments. For novelty analysis, LoGiPT is a weak direct threat to context construction because it intentionally removes the explicit parsing, selection, and external-prover stages; it is nonetheless relevant as an alternative architecture for formal-syntax adherence and logical execution. A system whose novelty depends on explicit argument-form extraction, proof-relevant context selection, or prover-verified construction remains distinguishable from LoGiPT’s end-to-end solver imitation.

## Cross-paper novelty conclusion

The retained literature separates into three context regimes. Explanation-Refiner is the closest precedent for selecting relevant, sufficient, necessary, redundant, and unrelated natural-language facts and converting the selected material into a prover-checked theory. Logic-LM and LINC are the main precedents for converting whole natural-language logical problems into formal premises and conclusions, while MALLS/LogicLLaMA strengthens the sentence-level translation component. LeanDojo/ReProver, Magnushammer, and Lean Copilot are the principal formal-library premise-retrieval precedents. Logic-LM++, Automated Theorem Provers Help Improve LLM Reasoning, LeanReasoner, and LoGiPT cover refinement, correction, Lean formalization, and solver imitation, respectively. A differentiated contribution should therefore state whether its novelty lies in a new argument-form taxonomy, extraction of argumentative relations rather than sentence-level FOL translation, proof-relevance selection under an explicit sufficiency/minimality/coverage/faithfulness objective, a cross-domain or long-context setting, or a new interface from selected natural-language context to formal verification; it should compare directly against Explanation-Refiner, Logic-LM/LINC, and at least one formal-premise retriever.
